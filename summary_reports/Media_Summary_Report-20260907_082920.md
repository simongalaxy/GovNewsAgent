### Topic: Food Safety and Environmental Hygiene – Overall Policy Direction  

**Background**  
The Environment and Ecology Bureau (EEB) is responsible for safeguarding Hong Kong’s food safety and public‑health environment.  Its remit includes legislation, inspection, and the promotion of a hygienic food supply chain while supporting the growth of local agriculture, fisheries and the broader food‑industry sector.  

**Chronological History**  
- **2025 (2nd‑stage legislative amendment)** – Strengthened deterrence powers for the Food and Environmental Hygiene Department (FEHD); provisions took effect **August 2025**.  
- **Jan 2026 – Feb 2026** – FEHD introduced AI‑enabled rodent‑tracking cameras and launched the **Anti‑rodent Charter** (covering ~720 000 households by Feb 2026).  
- **Feb 2026** – Joint Office for Investigation of Water‑Seepage Complaints (Buildings Dept. + FEHD) announced new investigative procedures to be rolled out in H1 2026.  
- **April 1 2026** – “Joint‑vetting” mechanism for outside‑seating applications of restaurants went live.  
- **April 15 2026** – Secretary for Environment and Ecology, Mr Tse Chin‑wan, delivered opening remarks at a LegCo Finance Committee special meeting outlining the above measures and the Blueprint for Sustainable Development of Agriculture & Fisheries (15th Five‑Year Plan).  

**Government Stand**  
- The EEB emphasizes a **risk‑based, technology‑enabled** approach to food safety and environmental hygiene, asserting that recent legislative amendments and operational tools (AI cameras, joint‑vetting, water‑seepage office) have already yielded measurable improvements (e.g., >80 % of 49 flagged rodent sites improved in H1 2025).  
- It maintains that streamlined licensing and the “joint‑vetting” process will **facilitate trade** while preserving safety standards.  

**Opposite Views**  
- **Industry stakeholders** have voiced concerns that accelerated licence processing could **reduce inspection depth**, potentially compromising safety.  
- **Resident groups** worry that AI‑based rodent monitoring may raise **privacy issues** and that the anti‑rodent charter’s reliance on property‑management cooperation could unevenly burden smaller landlords.  
- **Environmental NGOs** caution that increased intensification of agriculture/fisheries (as per the 15th Five‑Year Plan) may lead to **ecosystem stress** if not matched with robust environmental safeguards.  



### Topic: Rodent Prevention and Control  

**Background**  
Rodent infestations in high‑density urban areas present a persistent public‑health risk, contaminating food and spreading disease.  FEHD’s mandate includes surveillance, control, and public education.  

**Chronological History**  
- **2025 H1** – FEHD identified **49 high‑risk locations** with active rodent activity.  
- **2025 Aug** – Second‑stage legislative amendment on deterrence/enforcement came into force.  
- **2025 H2 – 2026 Feb** – Deployment of AI‑enabled cameras; **>80 %** of the 49 sites showed improvement.  
- **2026 Feb** – Launch of **Anti‑rodent Charter** covering **≈720 000 households**; collaboration with property‑management firms began.  
- **2026 H1** – Ongoing **Cross‑sectoral Territory‑wide Anti‑rodent Action** coordinated among multiple government bureaus.  

**Government Stand**  
- The bureau declares the AI‑camera system and charter as **effective deterrents**, citing quantitative improvements and enhanced inter‑agency coordination.  
- It stresses continued **investment in technology and cross‑sectoral action** to achieve a “rodent‑free environment.”  

**Opposite Views**  
- **Local merchants** argue that AI surveillance may **infringe on commercial privacy** and that compliance costs (e.g., installing cameras) are burdensome for small businesses.  
- **Animal‑rights advocates** contend that rodent control methods must adhere to humane standards, urging the government to disclose the specific control techniques used.  



### Topic: Water‑Seepage Complaint Investigation  

**Background**  
Water seepage in residential and commercial buildings can cause structural damage and health hazards.  Historically, investigations were fragmented between the Buildings Department and the FEHD.  

**Chronological History**  
- **2025** – Recognition of slow resolution times for water‑seepage complaints.  
- **2026 H1** – Formation of the **Joint Office for Investigation of Water Seepage Complaints** (Buildings Dept. + FEHD).  
- **2026 Feb** – Announcement of new, technology‑assisted procedures intended to **expedite source identification**; rollout scheduled for the first half of 2026.  
- **2026 H2 onward** – Government to strengthen **coordination and mediation** for dispute resolution between owners and landlords.  

**Government Stand**  
- The EEB asserts that the joint office will **streamline investigations**, reduce repair delays, and improve public health outcomes.  

**Opposite Views**  
- **Home‑owner associations** fear that the new procedures may **prioritise speed over thoroughness**, potentially overlooking structural issues.  
- **Legal commentators** point out that without clear statutory backing, the mediation mechanism could **undermine owners’ rights** to pursue independent legal action.  



### Topic: Market Modernisation Programme (MM‑P)  

**Background**  
Hong Kong’s traditional wet markets require upgrading to meet modern hygiene standards, improve shopper experience, and support local food vendors.  

**Chronological History**  
- **2019‑2022** – Initial rollout of the MM‑P, beginning with the **Queen Street Cooked Food Market** (positive feedback).  
- **2024‑2025** – FEHD identified three additional cooked‑food centres for renovation: **Ap Lei Chau**, **Luen Wo Hui**, and **Yue Wan**.  
- **2026 Throughout the year** – Construction and renovation of the three new centres scheduled for **gradual completion**.  

**Government Stand**  
- The bureau frames MM‑P as a **continuous investment** enhancing market hygiene, vendor profitability, and consumer satisfaction.  

**Opposite Views**  
- **Street vendors** worry that renovation periods may **displace livelihoods** and that rent hikes post‑renovation could be unaffordable.  
- **Heritage groups** argue that extensive modernization may **erase traditional market character** valued by locals and tourists.  



### Topic: Public Niche Allocation & Crematorium Development  

**Background**  
Hong Kong’s public columbarium niche supply has traditionally been allocated on an annual basis, causing inflexibility for families wishing to complete after‑death arrangements promptly.  

**Chronological History**  
- **2024‑2025** – Review of niche allocation showed **adequate overall supply** but highlighted **temporal mismatches**.  
- **2026 Aug** – Transition of three columbaria (Shek Mun, Wo Hop Shek, Cape Collinson‑San Ha) from **yearly to monthly** niche allocation, mirroring the model at Tsang Tsui.  
- **2026 H2** – Pending Finance Committee approval for **new crematorium construction at Wo Hop Shek Cemetery** to increase cremation capacity.  

**Government Stand**  
- The EEB states that monthly allocation **enhances flexibility** for families and that the new crematorium will **meet rising demand**.  

**Opposite Views**  
- **Family advocacy groups** worry that monthly allocations may **accelerate niche depletion**, potentially increasing future scarcity.  
- **Fiscal watchdogs** question the **cost‑effectiveness** of new crematorium construction given existing capacity.  



### Topic: Agriculture & Fisheries Development  

**Background**  
Hong Kong aims to modernise its modest local agriculture and fisheries sectors, aligning with the national **15th Five‑Year Plan** and the **Blueprint for Sustainable Development of Agriculture & Fisheries**.  

**Chronological History**  
- **2025** – AFCD issued the **first marine fish‑culture licences** for new zones at **Wong Chuk Kok Hoi** and **Mirs Bay**; deep‑sea cage rentals began.  
- **2025‑2026** – Target set to raise local mariculture output to **1 200 t by 2027** (future goal **≈6 000 t by 2037**).  
- **2026 Feb** – **165** fisheries/farms participated in “Agri enJoy” & “Fish enJoy” schemes.  
- **2026 H1** – Commencement of site‑formation works for the **first multi‑storey, environmentally‑friendly pig farm** in Lo Wu (completion due 2026).  
- **2026 Mid‑year** – AFCD preparing a **new unified branding** for local agri‑fisheries products (launch expected **mid‑2026**).  

**Government Stand**  
- The EEB stresses that these measures will **increase self‑sufficiency**, improve product quality, and create **higher income** for practitioners, while supporting national development goals.  

**Opposite Views**  
- **Environmental NGOs** raise concerns about **intensive aquaculture** and **multi‑storey pig farms** potentially worsening water pollution and waste management.  
- **Small‑scale farmers** fear that a **unified brand** could marginalise niche producers lacking certification resources.  



### Topic: Food Industry Promotion & Export Support  

**Background**  
Hong Kong’s food industry seeks to leverage its reputation for quality to expand into Mainland China and overseas markets.  

**Chronological History**  
- **2025‑2026** – Ongoing negotiations with Mainland authorities on **market‑access and customs simplifications** for Hong Kong‑manufactured food.  
- **2026 Apr 1** – CFS waived **certification fees** for food‑product certification for a **two‑year period** to lower compliance costs.  
- **2026 H1** – EEB began a **“Food from Hong Kong” branding campaign** and pursued cross‑border collaborations to position Hong Kong as a **food hub**.  

**Government Stand**  
- The bureau claims that fee waivers, branding, and trade talks will **boost exports**, reduce compliance burdens, and align with the dual‑flow economic strategy of the 15th Five‑Year Plan.  

**Opposite Views**  
- **Local SMEs** argue that **branding alone** cannot compensate for structural challenges such as limited production capacity and high operating costs.  
- **Consumer advocates** warn that fee waivers might reduce **regulatory rigor**, potentially affecting product safety standards.

### Topic: Regulation of Dogs in Food Premises (Policy Development, Implementation & Enforcement)  

**Background**  
The Food Business (Amendment) Regulation 2026 was drafted to **legalise the controlled entry of pet dogs** (excluding guide/working dogs) into designated restaurants, aiming to meet growing public demand for pet‑friendly dining while safeguarding food safety and hygiene.  

**Chronological History**  

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| **2025 Oct** | Legislative amendment submitted to LegCo (allowing dogs in restaurants – first‑phase applications expected mid‑2026). |
| **2026 Apr 15** | Secretary for Environment & Ecology, Mr Tse Chin‑wan, announced upcoming joint‑vetting mechanism for outside‑seating licences and reiterated support for the dog‑entry measure. |
| **2026 May 7** | FEHD announced that the **Food Business (Amendment) Regulation 2026** will take effect **May 8**; applications open **May 18**. |
| **2026 May 11‑13** | First three **briefing sessions** for the catering trade (150+ attendees) explaining application procedures, eligibility, and licensing conditions. |
| **2026 May 18 – Jun 8** | **Application window** opened; electronic submissions via dedicated webpage. **Quota:** ≤ 1 000 restaurants (first phase). Restrictions: no hot‑pot/barbecue venues; minimum floor area 20 m². |
| **2026 May 22** | **Guidelines on Good Practices & Behaviour** released (three‑part document covering operator duties, customer conduct, and interaction etiquette). |
| **2026 May 28** | Fourth briefing session held; cumulative **≥ 360** in‑person attendees, **≈ 17 000** online viewers. |
| **2026 Jun 6** | Reminder issued: applications must be submitted **by 23:59 on Jun 8**. > 2 000 applications received by that date. |
| **2026 Jun 9** | Announcement of **ballot** to allocate the 1 000 quotas; **2 205** applications deemed valid after filtering. |
| **2026 Jun 12** | **Ballot conducted**; 1 000 successful applicants announced; **first batch** of licences to be amended (fee $140) by **Jun 24**. |
| **2026 Jun 26** | List of **833** restaurants that completed amendment procedures released; waiting‑list applicants earmarked for vacant quotas. |
| **2026 Jul 9** | **Dogs permitted to enter** the first batch of **≈ 940** (later > 970) approved restaurants. FEHD deployed **90‑member dedicated team** for on‑site guidance. |
| **2026 Jul 11** | FEHD clarified **application eligibility** (premises must legally allow dogs) and **operational status** of listed restaurants (addressing media reports of closed venues). |
| **2026 Jul 14** | First **warning letter** issued to a permitted restaurant (Kowloon City) for repeated breach of the “no cooking on dining tables” rule. |
| **2026 Jul 17** | **Targeted enforcement** launched against non‑permitted premises; **first prosecution** of a non‑permitted restaurant in Tsuen Wan. |
| **2026 Jul 25** | Mid‑adaptation review: **≈ 970** permitted venues; waiting‑list mechanism operating smoothly; government stresses “advice before enforcement” stance. |
| **2026 Aug 9** | End of one‑month adaptation period: **≈ 980** permitted venues; **≈ 29 600** inspections conducted, **77** verbal warnings, **3** warning letters, **2** prosecutions (permitted) and **6** prosecutions (non‑permitted). |
| **2026 Aug 9 onward** | FEHD to shift to **risk‑based flexible inspections**; enforcement without prior warning for serious breaches. |

**Government Stand**  
- The FEHD (and EEB) present the dog‑entry scheme as a **balanced, risk‑based policy** that respects **public demand for pet‑friendly dining** while maintaining **food safety, hygiene and public order**.  
- It stresses **transparent, technology‑supported processes** (online applications, public ballot, searchable list of permitted venues, clear guidelines).  
- Enforcement philosophy: **“advice before enforcement”** during the adaptation period, with **swift punitive action** (warning letters, licence revocation, fines, imprisonment) for persistent or serious non‑compliance.  

**Opposite Views**  
- **Catering trade**: Some operators voiced **operational concerns**—additional staff training, layout redesign, and potential liability for dog‑related incidents; a minority questioned the **quota limitation (1 000)** as insufficient for market demand.  
- **Animal‑welfare organisations**: While supportive of pet inclusion, they urged the government to ensure **humane handling** of dogs, proper **leash policies**, and **adequate ventilation** to protect animal welfare.  
- **Public‑health advocates**: Highlighted risks of **cross‑contamination** and **allergen exposure**, calling for stricter **monitoring of cooking on tables** and clearer **segregation** of dog‑friendly zones.  
- **Legal commentators**: Warned that the requirement for operators to verify that building leases permit dog entry could lead to **contractual disputes** and **potential liability** for false statements on application forms.  
- **Non‑permitted restaurant owners**: Some expressed dissatisfaction with **penalties for accidental breaches**, arguing that the rapid rollout left insufficient time for **staff education** and **infrastructure adaptation**.
