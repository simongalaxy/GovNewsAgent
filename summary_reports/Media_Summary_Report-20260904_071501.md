**Media Summary Report – “Dogs in Food Premises” Policy (April – August 2026)**  

*Executive Overview*  
In early 2026 the Hong Kong Government introduced a regulatory framework permitting dogs to enter selected restaurants. The initiative, announced by the Environment and Ecology Bureau (EEB) and implemented by the Food and Environmental Hygiene Department (FEHD), progressed through legislative amendment, a joint‑vetting licence mechanism, a public application and ballot process, the issuance of operational Guidelines, and a month‑long adaptation period accompanied by extensive inspections and enforcement actions. By early August 2026 the scheme was operating with roughly 980‑plus permitted premises, a functioning waiting‑list system, and a clear enforcement stance toward non‑compliant operators.

## 2026‑04‑15 – Opening Remarks by the Secretary for Environment and Ecology (Mr Tse Chin‑wan)

### 1. Environmental‑hygiene initiatives  
- Completed the **second‑stage legislative amendment (2025)** to strengthen deterrence and enforcement; in force since August 2025.  
- **Rodent control**: AI‑enabled cameras deployed; 49 high‑risk sites identified in H1 2025, >80 % showed improvement.  
- **Anti‑rodent Charter**: covered ≈720,000 households (Feb 2026).  
- **Cross‑sectoral Territory‑wide Anti‑rodent Action** continued.  
- **Water‑seepage programme**: Joint Office (Buildings Dept. + FEHD) to introduce new investigation procedures in H1 2026, using technology to speed source identification and promote mediation.  

### 2. Food‑business regulation  
- **Joint‑vetting mechanism** for outside‑seating applications effective 1 April 2026; simple cases to be approved in ~1 month.  
- **Legislative amendment** submitted to LegCo to allow dogs in restaurants; first‑phase licences expected by mid‑2026.  
- **Food Surveillance Programme** continued under the Centre for Food Safety (CFS) with risk‑based sampling.  

### 3. Market modernisation & burial services  
- Ongoing **Market Modernisation Programme** – renovations at Ap Lei Chau, Luen Wo Hui, and Yue Wan cooked‑food centres slated for completion within 2026.  
- Public niche allocation changed from yearly to **monthly allocation (effective Aug 2026)** at Shek Mun, Wo Hop Shek, and Cape Collinson‑San Ha columbaria.  

### 4. Agriculture & fisheries development  
- New **marine fish‑culture zones** (Wong Chuk Kok Hoi & Mirs Bay) licensed in 2025; target production 1,200 t by 2027, 6,000 t by 2037.  
- **Multi‑storey pig farm (Lo Wu)** site formation to finish 2026; applications under assessment.  
- **Agri‑enJoy / Fish‑enJoy Schemes**: 165 eligible farms/fisheries (Feb 2026).  
- Preparation of a **unified brand** for local produce (mid‑2026).  

### 5. Food‑industry development  
- Negotiations with Mainland authorities for market access and simplified customs.  
- **CFS fee waiver** for food‑product certification (2‑year period starting 1 April 2026).  
- Promotion of “Food from Hong Kong” brand and Hong Kong as an international food hub.

## 2026‑05‑07 – commencement of applications for dog‑entry licences  

- **Regulatory change**: *Food Business (Amendment) Regulation 2026* took effect 8 May 2026.  
- **Application window**: 18 May – 8 June 2026 (electronic submission via FEHD website).  
- **Quota**: maximum 1,000 restaurants in the first phase; excess applications to be allocated by ballot.  
- **Exclusions**: hot‑pot, barbecue (including teppanyaki, Korean) restaurants; premises <20 m².  
- **Fees**: HK$140 licence amendment fee per successful applicant.  
- **Briefing sessions**: four scheduled (11 May, 12 May, 13 May, 28 May).  
- **Publicity**: dedicated hotlines (2867 5912 / 2867 2836) opened 8 May.

## 2026‑06‑26 – Update on first‑batch compliance  

- **Status as of 25 June**: 833 of the successful applicants had completed all required steps; dogs permitted from 9 July.  
- **Waiting‑list activation**: vacancies filled from ballot order; new approvals being issued.  
- **Continuing support**: FEHD officers visiting premises to brief operators and verify dog‑entry feasibility under lease/tenancy terms.

## 2026‑07‑10 (reported 11 July) – Media enquiry response on eligibility & operational status  

- **Eligibility emphasis**: Applicants must confirm that the premises’ tenancy/deed permits dog entry; false declarations may lead to suspension or revocation.  
- **Operational verification**: FEHD validated licences at ballot time and conducted site visits; closures, renovations, or transfers are normal market occurrences.  
- **Quota reallocation**: If a permitted premise ceases operation or withdraws, its quota passes to the next wait‑list applicant, with updates posted on the FEHD portal.

---

## 2026‑07‑14 – First enforcement warning issued  

- **Target**: a permitted Chinese restaurant in Kowloon City District (non‑compliant with prohibition on cooking/heating food at tables).  
- **Enforcement timeline**: verbal advice (11 July), verbal warning (12 July), repeat breach (13 July) → formal **warning letter** issued (14 July).  
- **Regulatory stance**: “Advice before enforcement” during the first month, but serious breaches may trigger decisive action, including fines, licence revocation, and possible imprisonment (up to HK$10,000, 3 months, daily fine HK$300).  
- **Inspection activity**: ~4,700 inspections of permitted premises and >400 of non‑permitted premises up to 13 July.

---

## 2026‑07‑17 – Targeted enforcement against non‑permitted premises  

- **Prosecution**: first case against a non‑permitted restaurant in Tsuen Wan (dogs allowed unlawfully).  
- **Inspection figures**: >780 inspections of non‑permitted premises; 10 verbal warnings issued.  
- **Continued permitted‑premise monitoring**: ~7,500 inspections; two additional warning letters (Chinese restaurant in Kowloon City, Thai restaurant in Tsuen Wan) for cooking on tables.  
- **Legal framework reiterated**: Food Business Regulation (Cap. 132X) penalties reaffirmed.

---

## 2026‑07‑25 – Mid‑adaptation period assessment (two weeks in)  

- **Public reception**: increasing acceptance; overall operation “smooth and stable.”  
- **Permitted‑premise count**: grew from ≈940 (9 July) to >970 (25 July).  
- **Waiting‑list mechanism**: functioning efficiently; vacancies promptly re‑allocated.  
- **Inspection activity**: 90‑officer team continued support, publicity, and education.  
- **Policy outlook**: “Advice before enforcement” will continue for the remaining adaptation period, with readiness to act on serious non‑compliance.

---

## 2026‑08‑09 – End of one‑month adaptation period (overall performance)  

- **Compliance level**: generally satisfactory; most operators complied after department assistance.  
- **Permitted‑premise count**: >980 restaurants.  
- **Inspection volume**: ~29,600 inspections of permitted restaurants; 77 verbal warnings; **three warning letters** for persistent cooking‑on‑tables violations.  
- **Enforcement actions**:  
  - **Two prosecutions** for failure to keep dogs on leashes.  
  - **Six prosecutions** of non‑permitted restaurants allowing dogs.  
- **Future approach**: post‑adaptation inspections will be risk‑based; any detected non‑compliance will trigger immediate enforcement without prior warning.  
- **Overall message**: FEHD urges both operators and dog owners to respect licensing conditions and mutual responsibilities to sustain a safe, hygienic, and pet‑inclusive dining environment.
